Little Red List

Write down what you want. Read it morning, noon and night.

A daily practice tool based on It Works, the short book published in 1926 by R. H. Jarrett. The app is independent and is not connected to any publisher of the book.

Get it on the App Store

What the app does

You keep a list of what you want, in the order it matters to you. The app reminds you three times a day — morning, noon and night — and walks you through your list one item at a time.

  • Your list, in your order, rewritten as often as you like. Every wording you have ever used is kept.
  • Three reminders a day, at times you choose. A reading you have already done is not reminded about again.
  • A record of the practice you have actually kept — including the readings that fall outside your chosen times.
  • Somewhere to keep the things that came about, with the thanks you wrote for them.
  • Optional waves, rain or static to read to, made by the app as it plays.

The ideas behind the practice are the author's, set out in his book. The app is a tool for keeping the practice, and it makes no promises about what follows from it.

Support

If something is broken, or the app does something you did not expect, please write to littleredlist.app@gmail.com. It is read by a person.

What helps when reporting a problem

  • What you were doing, and what happened instead.
  • Which screen you were on.
  • Your iPhone model and iOS version — Settings → General → About.
  • A screenshot, if the problem is something you can see.

The app keeps no logs and sends nothing anywhere, so a description from you is the only way a problem can be found. That is the trade for an app that watches nothing.

Asking for something new

Requests are welcome at the same address. Saying what you were trying to do is more useful than naming a feature — there is often a simpler answer than the one either of us would have thought of first.

Privacy policy

Effective 1 October 2026.

Little Red List collects nothing about you. There is no account to make, no sign-in, and nothing to switch off.

What stays on your iPhone

Everything you write — your list, every wording it has had, your thanks, your reading times and your settings — is stored on your iPhone and nowhere else. It is never sent to us or to anybody else.

What the app does not do

  • It has no accounts and no sign-in.
  • It contains no analytics, no tracking and no advertising.
  • It contains no third-party code of any kind.
  • It makes no network connections. The app works the same with the phone in aeroplane mode.

Your backups

If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, your list is part of that backup, in the same way your notes and photos are. Those backups are handled by Apple under Apple's terms, not by us, and we cannot read them.

Crash reports

If you have chosen to share diagnostics with developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may pass on a report when an app closes unexpectedly. Those reports come from Apple, describe only what the software was doing at the time, and contain nothing you have written.

Children

The app is not directed at children, and since it collects nothing, it collects nothing about children either.

Deleting everything

Settings inside the app has a Start over option that erases everything the app has saved. Deleting the app from your iPhone removes it all as well. Because nothing was ever sent anywhere, there is nothing else to ask us to delete.

Changes to this policy

If this ever changes, the new version will appear on this page with a new date at the top.

Getting in touch

About the book

It Works was published in 1926 by R. H. Jarrett, who wrote under the initials RHJ. It is a pamphlet of a few thousand words, and it asks for one thing: write down what you want, in order of importance, and read that list three times a day.

The book is not inside the app, and the passages the app shows are short extracts. A scan of the whole thing is free to read at the Internet Archive.
